# Designing a case to practice a skill

Map each skill to a case feature the student must handle:

| Skill | Put this in the case |
|---|---|
| Earning trust | A reluctant critical fact: an embarrassing habit (skipping a medicine, drinking, smoking, not affording pills). He jokes or changes the subject until asked without judgment. |
| Covering the basics | An `asked` critical fact behind a routine question nobody skips in real life: over-the-counter pills, who manages medicines, what he eats. |
| Complete handoffs | 4-5 critical facts so a short SBAR loses something. Make one of them sound minor but change the plan. |
| Plain-language teaching | Plan points with numbers and timing (weights, doses, cups per day). A caregiver with low health literacy. |
| Avoiding jargon | Plan points where the obvious medical word must be avoided (diuretic, sodium, inhaler technique, glucose). |
| Working with interpreters | A caregiver who needs an interpreter, and plan points with numbers and units that can change in translation. |

Rules:
- Use a different patient, condition, and caregiver language than the student's last case.
- Keep the difficulty fair: every hidden fact must be reachable with one good question (write it in `unlock_hint`).
- Write `/notes/design.md`: a short table of skill → case feature, so the professor can see the intent.
